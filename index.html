Mei Yang

MA Student, Computational Social Science · University of Chicago

meiyang@uchicago.edu

Chicago, IL

I am a first-year MA student in Computational Social Science at the University of Chicago, where I am supported by the Midway Research and MA Director’s Scholarships. My research sits at the intersection of computational modeling, social psychology, and behavioral economics, with a focus on cooperation, decision-making, and human well-being.

Before UChicago, I completed an Honours BSc in Psychology at the University of Toronto (GPA 3.88). There I worked with Dr. Dirk Bernhardt-Walther on the empirical aesthetics of bridge design—asking what structural features make bridges beautiful—and with Dr. Felix Cheung modeling individual well-being trajectories from 84-day experience-sampling data using machine learning. My senior thesis, advised by Dr. Will Cunningham, used multi-agent reinforcement learning to model coordination and power dynamics in hierarchical groups.

Outside of research, I am an avid film lover—my favorite directors are Wong Kar-wai, Ang Lee, and Quentin Tarantino. I am also the proud owner of a Bichon Frise named 发财 (meaning “prosperity” in Mandarin).

  1. Sci. Adv.
    Epilogue to the war: Afghanistan reports the lowest well-being in recorded history
    Levi D. Stutzman, Polin Lun, Mei Yang, and 2 more authors
    Science Advances, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML

    Using nationally representative survey data collected after the 2021 US withdrawal from Afghanistan, we document that Afghans report the lowest life satisfaction ever recorded in global well-being data—lower than any country measured since systematic collection began. These findings provide quantitative evidence of the severe human cost of prolonged conflict and political instability, and call for targeted international attention to post-conflict recovery.

  2. JEP:HPP
    A large positive hysteresis effect for scene categories
    He Chen, Mei Yang, Gwidon Son, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance, 2025
    Abs Bib HTML

    When does the visual system commit to perceiving one scene category over another? We demonstrate a large positive hysteresis effect: the scene category you perceived most recently biases your interpretation of a subsequent ambiguous scene, and this history-dependence is far stronger than prior estimates suggested. The results reveal that visual scene categorization is not a stateless, bottom-up process—it is deeply shaped by recent perceptual history.

  3. PLOS ONE
    Empirical aesthetics of bridges
    Mei Yang, Claudia Damiano, Paul Gauvreau, and 1 more author
    PLOS ONE, 2025
    Media coverage: UofT Psychology
    Abs Bib HTML

    Bridges serve dual purposes as functional infrastructure and aesthetic objects. We had 254 online participants rate 491 bridge images on aesthetic pleasure, visual interest, complexity, and perceived safety, while civil engineers annotated each bridge’s structural type, material, age, and aesthetic investment. Factor analysis revealed two principal dimensions: an "aesthetics" dimension linking pleasure, interest, and complexity to bridge type, and a "perceived safety" dimension linking safety to materials and age. Contour analysis further showed that cable-stayed and suspension bridges—with shorter, more angular contours—receive higher aesthetic ratings than slab bridges. This study offers systematic empirical data to ground bridge design decisions in public aesthetic experience.
